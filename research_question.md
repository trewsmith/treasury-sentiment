# Research Question



How quickly and under what market conditions is textual financial information incorporated into U.S. Treasury yields, and does social media provide incremental explanatory power beyond professional financial news?

## Motivation

Financiial markets continuously process information from a wide range of sources, but the speed and extent to which textual information is incorporated into Treasury prices remain less well understood at an intraday frequency. Existing research has established relationships between news sentiment and interest rates, while other work has examined the information content of social media and the role of market regimes. This project brings these dimensions together by examining the timing, content, and source of textual information associated with movements in the U.S. Treasury market.

## Research Objectives

The study will examine whether different categories of financial information are associated with subsequent movements in the 10-year Treasury yield, how quickly these relationships emerge across 5-minute, 30-minute, and 60-minute horizons, and whether they vary across different market conditions. It will also test whether information from social media provides explanatory power beyond information contained in professional financial news.

## Information Categories

Rather than relying solely on general positive or negative sentiment, the analysis will classify financial information according to its economic implications, the categories probably being:

* Hawkish or dovish
* Inflationary or disinflationary
* Growth or recessionary
* Risk-on or risk-off
* Fiscal and Treasury supply

## Empirical Framework

The analysis will use professional financial news and Reddit posts alongside intraday movements in the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield. The study will control for other major market variables, including the S&P 500, VIX, DXY, oil prices, the 2-year Treasury yield, and major macroeconomic events. Regression, lagged, event-study, and regime-based analyses will be used to evaluate the timing and robustness of these relationships.

## Expected Contribution

The project aims to extend existing research on news sentiment and interest rates by examining the speed of textual information incorporation, distinguishing between economically meaningful information categories, comparing professional news with social media, and evaluating whether these relationships differ across market regimes.
